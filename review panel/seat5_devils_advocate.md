### Genuine Strengths

Brief and honest, per Review Discipline 5 — these are real and I am not manufacturing balance beyond them.

- The paper reports a negative result plainly and repeatedly (§IV-B: CGVD "consistently underperforms the baseline" on Carrot-on-Plate; Table I caption concedes non-monotonic variance at low distractor counts). Most submissions in this space would bury both.
- The evaluation scale is genuinely unusual for this literature: 200 episodes per condition with seeds matched between baseline and CGVD, ~19,200 episodes for Fig. 3 alone.
- The mean-color-fill ablation is a control most authors would omit, and it is the single row that most damages the paper's own narrative. It was run and reported anyway.
- The pipeline is described at a level of block-diagram specificity that a competent group could re-implement, modulo the missing hyperparameters (MN2).

### Strongest Counter-Argument

Suppose I hold that CGVD demonstrates nothing about attention and nothing about clutter robustness. My case is built entirely from the authors' own numbers.

First, the mechanism. The Abstract asserts that failure "is driven by background-induced feature dilution." Table II refutes it. Replacing LaMa with a mean-color fill removes exactly the same semantic content — identical masks, identical objects gone — yet 21.0 of the 34.5-point gain evaporates. Sixty-one percent of CGVD's benefit is therefore attributable to how the hole is painted, not to what was removed. That is a distribution-conformity effect on a ViT, which the authors themselves name ("adversarial patches"), not a semantic-dilution effect.

Second, the residual 13.5 points need no attention theory either. Under semantic clutter the documented failure is grasping a spatula instead of a spoon. Deleting the spatula deletes the wrong-grasp option. Success rises for the same reason a multiple-choice score rises when distractor answers are struck out. Fig. 4 cannot distinguish this from "attention repair": erasing an object necessarily erases attention on it, so the figure's observation is entailed by the intervention under every hypothesis, including the null.

Third, one hypothesis — restoring the observation toward the clutter-sparse Bridge distribution — explains all of it, including the case CGVD loses. On Carrot-on-Plate the authors invoke distribution-matching to explain why clutter helps the baseline, then abandon it to explain why removing clutter helps elsewhere. One mechanism, two directions.

Fourth, the distractor vocabulary D is never derived. If it names the injected categories, the experiment measures removal of a known perturbation, not open-world robustness.

None of this requires doubting a single reported number.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Training-distribution restoration.** CGVD's operation is, mechanically, "return the observation to something closer to the clutter-sparse Bridge scenes the policies were trained on." This single hypothesis predicts the mean-color-fill collapse (an unnatural patch is further out of distribution than a LaMa fill, despite removing identical semantics), the Carrot-on-Plate loss (Bridge carrot scenes are object-rich, so removal moves *away* from the training distribution), and the authors' own §IV-B explanation of why clutter helps the baseline. It is never named or tested, and the decisive control is cheap: a texture-matched fill, or an inpaint-and-reinsert condition.
2. **Choice-set reduction, not attention repair.** The documented semantic-clutter failure is grasping the spatula instead of the spoon. Deleting the spatula deletes the wrong-grasp option. This requires no representational claim whatsoever, and it makes the contribution a perception-engineering result — "can you delete the confusable object without deleting the target" — rather than a claim about VLA internals. The paper's own MJ1 admission puts the answer at "probabilistically."
3. **Low-level statistics.** LaMa output is smooth and low-texture. If reduced high-frequency content is the operative variable, a Gaussian blur of the distractor regions — orders of magnitude cheaper than SAM3 plus LaMa, and requiring no vocabulary D — should recover much of the gain. Untested.
4. **Oracle removal as an upper bound.** SimplerEnv can render the scene with distractors physically absent. That single condition would bound CGVD's headroom and cleanly separate "removal helps" from "CGVD's perception is good." Its absence means perception error and intervention effect are never disentangled.
5. **Cheaper or more mature alternative paths not compared:** crop-and-zoom to the target region (the coarse-to-fine idea the paper cites as [17] and then abandons); desaturating or darkening distractors instead of erasing them, which preserves obstacle geometry and would address MJ10; bounding-box visual prompting ([23], cited and dismissed without test); and fine-tuning on inpainted observations, which would directly test hypothesis 1.
6. **The null path.** Given MJ7's unquantified noise floor and MJ8's absent tests, "the differences at low and moderate distractor counts are run-to-run variation" has not been excluded for most cells of Table I.

### Unexamined Premise

The paper never questions whether success rate measured on an *edited* observation is the same construct as robustness to clutter. Its entire frame assumes the correct locus of repair is the observation — that the ideal input to a VLA is a scene containing only task-relevant objects. But a deployed manipulator cannot pre-edit the world, and the robustness has not been created so much as relocated: it now lives in SAM3's recall over D and LaMa's fidelity, neither of which is measured anywhere in the paper. The system is thus evaluated on a task it partially defines. Every reported success rate is the rate of the composite system on episodes where the perception layer happened to work, with no decomposition into "removal helped" versus "removal succeeded." The two failure modes that must occur at nonzero rate in any open world — a missed distractor and an erased target — have no measured rate, no reported example, and no entry in §V. Until that decomposition exists, "CGVD improves clutter robustness" and "CGVD's perception worked on these scenes" are indistinguishable statements about the same numbers.
