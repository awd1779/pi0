### Summary Assessment

The paper proposes CGVD, a training-free inference-time wrapper that parses a manipulation instruction into a safe set and a distractor set, segments both with SAM3, subtracts the safe set from the distractor mask, and inpaints the remainder with LaMa before the observation reaches a frozen VLA policy. Evaluated in SimplerEnv on two Bridge tasks with pi0 and GR00T, it reports substantial gains under semantic and attribute clutter and an honest loss under contextually informative clutter.

The engineering is sensible and the two-layer genuineness refinement is a genuine, well-targeted fix to a real open-set segmentation failure mode. The paper's problem is not its system but its claims. §I and §II-D define the contribution entirely against three named prior methods — BYOVLA [9], DTP [30], OBEYED-VLA [8] — and §IV then compares only against un-augmented pi0, leaving the Abstract's "outperforms state-of-the-art baselines" with no empirical referent. The named mechanism ("attention repair", feature dilution) is asserted across four sections and supported by one qualitative figure with no attention metric. The provenance of the distractor vocabulary D is never stated, which leaves open whether the headline comparison is oracle-assisted.

These are repairable, and the honestly reported carrot-on-plate negative result is a credit to the authors. Major Revision.

### Strengths

1. **Honest reporting of a result that cuts against the paper's own thesis**: §IV-B states plainly that CGVD "consistently underperforms the baseline" on Carrot-on-Plate and offers a plausible account — that contextual clutter matches the pre-training distribution and supplies visual anchors for reasoning. In a subfield where negative conditions are routinely omitted, reporting this is research integrity and it is also the paper's most scientifically interesting observation. It should be promoted, not buried.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-B "when a task naturally benefits from contextual clutter, aggressively masking the scene deprives the VLA of useful environmental reasoning"`

2. **The genuineness differential is a correct diagnosis of a real open-set failure mode**: open-vocabulary detectors score text prompts independently, so a spatula legitimately returns high confidence for "spoon". Scoring the *differential* between safe-set and distractor identities, preserving negative values, and folding them into a connected-component score is a cheap and appropriate fix that does not require a VLM call. The worked spatula example makes the logic checkable.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `equation: Eq. (4) — the composite component score (1 + g*) · σ* that penalizes low-genuineness imposters`

3. **The distractor-type taxonomy and the compositional-prompt experiment are the right conceptual axes**: separating semantic, random, and attribute distractors, and then crossing attribute distractors with simple vs. compositional prompt structures, isolates exactly the failure mode the clutter-robustness literature cares about. Table I's complex-prompt column is the paper's most convincing evidence and would remain valuable independent of the CGVD method itself.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table I — Complex Prompt column, pi0 falls 85.0 to 57.0 across 0-4 distractors while CGVD holds 87.0 to 73.0`

4. **Latency is reported at all, and the caching design is the right engineering answer**: front-loading segmentation and inpainting to t=0 and compositing thereafter is the correct structural choice for a perception wrapper, and reporting the initialization cost rather than hiding it is good practice for an inference-intervention paper.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table III — Initialization 4,914 ms at t=0 versus 421 ms per-step execution, showing the cost is front-loaded`

5. **The static-background assumption is stated as an assumption rather than glossed**: §V identifies the desynchronization failure mode explicitly and explains why real-time mask updating is currently impractical, rather than presenting the cached design as free.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §V "If a distractor is moved dynamically, the cached background will desynchronize from the physical scene"`

### Weaknesses

1. **The comparative claim has no empirical referent**: §I builds the entire motivation by distinguishing CGVD from three named families — adaptation (OBEYED-VLA [8]), inference-time intervention (BYOVLA [9]), and training-time augmentation [10]-[12] — and §II-D adds DTP [30]. §IV then compares only against un-augmented pi0 and GR00T. The "baseline" in "77.5% compared to the baseline's 43.0%" is an ablation baseline, not a state-of-the-art method, so the Abstract's "significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines" is not tested anywhere in the paper. This is not a norm-based objection; it is the paper asserting a comparative result it does not run. BYOVLA [9] is the one that matters most: it is the same intervention family (run-time observation intervention with distractor removal), it is ICRA-published, and the manuscript critiques its cost profile and its "probabilistic protection" in prose while never measuring either. A CGVD-vs-BYOVLA table on Spoon-on-Towel under semantic clutter — success rate plus wall-clock — would settle both the accuracy and the efficiency arguments at once. If DTP [30] (a 2026 preprint) and OBEYED-VLA [8] have no public implementation, say so in one sentence; silence reads as omission rather than unavailability.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §IV Experiments — expected a head-to-head comparison against BYOVLA [9], DTP [30], or OBEYED-VLA [8]; checked Tables I-III, Fig. 3 caption, §IV-A protocol, §V`
   **Severity**: Critical | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: I work directly in this inference-time-intervention line and the absence is verifiable from the manuscript's own tables
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — add BYOVLA [9] as an empirical baseline on at least the semantic-clutter condition, and add an explicit availability statement for [30] and [8]. Failing that, remove "state-of-the-art baselines" from the Abstract and state the comparison as against un-augmented policies.

2. **The provenance of the distractor set D is never stated, and the "instruction determines the gate" framing covers only half the gate**: §III-B derives the safe set from the instruction (c_tgt, c_anc) and then introduces D = {d_1, ..., d_K} as "semantic categories that may appear as clutter (e.g., spatula, fork, knife)". Eq. (1) unions SAM3 over each d_k ∈ D, so D must be an enumerated prompt list — but the instruction does not contain it, and the paper never says where it comes from. Three consequences follow. First, §III's key insight ("a language instruction already specifies which objects matter") is only half true. Second, §III-B's differentiator over [9] — "our parsing is deterministic and requires no additional API" — is incomplete, since it accounts for the safe set only. Third, and most seriously, if D is populated from the ground-truth list of injected RoboCasa/YCB categories, then CGVD holds privileged scene information the baseline does not, and the 77.5 vs 43.0 comparison is oracle-assisted. As written, a reader cannot tell. This alone blocks acceptance because the headline number's validity turns on it.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §III-B and §III-C — expected a statement of how the distractor category list D is enumerated at inference; checked §III-B, §III-C, Eq. (1), §IV-A setup, §V`
   **Severity**: Critical | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: open-vocabulary prompt-set construction for manipulation is directly in my area, and the gap is established from the manuscript text alone
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — state exactly how D is obtained. If it is a fixed generic vocabulary, give it and show robustness to vocabulary choice. If it is derived per-episode, say by what. If it is the injected-object list, report a second condition with a task-agnostic D so the improvement can be attributed to CGVD rather than to scene knowledge.

3. **The paper's named mechanism is asserted throughout and measured nowhere**: "Precision-Reasoning Gap", "background-induced feature dilution", "attention corruption", and "attention leakage" appear in the Abstract, §I, §II-A and §II-D, and Fig. 4 promises "Attention Repair" with attention "forcing... to collapse onto the true target". The supporting evidence is one qualitative figure. The caption does not say which policy produced the maps (pi0 and GR00T have quite different backbones and attention structures), which layer or head, how heads were aggregated, or how many episodes the example represents. There is no attention-mass measurement on target vs. distractor regions. Consequently the experiments establish only "clutter → failure" and "CGVD → less failure"; they do not establish the intermediate attention step at all, and a simpler explanation — fewer objects in the scene means fewer competing grasp candidates — is not excluded. Since the mechanism is what names the phenomenon and justifies pixel-space over feature-space intervention, it needs either measurement or an explicit downgrade.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `figure: Fig. 4 (Top) and (Bottom) panels — attention maps presented with no model identity, layer, head, aggregation method, or attention-mass metric`
   **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: cross-attention behavior in ViT-backbone action models is my publication area
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** in one of two forms. Either (a) quantify: report normalized attention mass on target vs. distractor regions, aggregated over episodes with and without CGVD, stating model, layer, head-aggregation, and extraction method (attention rollout is the standard tool); or (b) downgrade explicitly — rename Fig. 4 as an illustrative hypothesis, and replace "forcing the attention mechanism to collapse" with hypothesis language throughout. Option (b) is a two-hour edit and costs the paper nothing it has actually earned.

4. **The mechanism vocabulary is internally contradictory and the information-bottleneck framing is metaphorical**: the Abstract attributes failure to "high-frequency semantic noise", §II-A and §II-C repeat "high-frequency background noise", and §II-B then describes CGVD as "a high-pass filter that blocks clutter". A high-pass filter passes high frequencies; blocking high-frequency content is low-pass behavior. Beyond the direction error, "high-frequency" is doing two incompatible jobs: literal spatial frequency (the sense in which LaMa's Fourier convolutions [14] operate) and a loose sense of "semantically dense". A large distractor object is low spatial frequency, so the literal reading does not describe the phenomenon. Separately, [24] applies an information bottleneck to latent redundancy in behavior cloning — a learned, training-time stochastic compression with a Lagrangian trade-off. CGVD is a hard, hand-specified pixel-space mask with no learned rate–distortion trade-off, so "acts as a semantic information bottleneck [24]" is analogy rather than framework application. This does not affect the success-rate results, but it does mean the paper's stated causal mechanism is not well defined as written.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §II-B "acts as a semantic information bottleneck [24], serving as a high-pass filter that blocks clutter"`
   **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: signal-processing and IB formalisms are standard in my area and the contradiction is internal to the text
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — pick one vocabulary. Either commit to the semantic framing and drop the frequency language entirely (recommended; it costs nothing), or define precisely which spatial-frequency band is being suppressed and correct the filter direction. Either present [24] as an analogy with that word, or apply the bottleneck formally (which would require a rate–distortion characterization the paper does not have).

5. **The causal account in §II-A is inconsistent with the policies actually evaluated, and its cited support is questionable**: §II-A attributes the geometric-grounding limitation "partly [to] the discretization of action spaces [2, 15]" — but neither evaluated policy discretizes actions. pi0 [5] is a flow-matching model with a continuous action head, and GR00T N1 [4] uses a diffusion transformer head. The named cause therefore does not apply to the systems under test. Separately, [18] (Naseer et al., *Intriguing properties of vision transformers*) is used twice, in §II-A to support "high-frequency background noise competes with task-relevant signals" and in §II-D to support ViT self-attention entanglement. My reading of [18] is that its headline results run the other way: ViTs are notably robust to severe occlusion and patch removal and are *less* reliant on high-frequency texture cues than CNNs. If [18] does contain a result supporting the manuscript's use, please quote it; if not, the mechanism paragraph currently has no literature anchor.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §II-A "This limitation stems partly from the discretization of action spaces [2, 15] and the static patch resolution"`
   **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise on pi0/GR00T action heads (confidence 5 on that half); the [18] assessment rests on recall rather than re-reading during this review
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — rewrite §II-A's causal account so the named causes apply to pi0 and GR00T (patch resolution and token competition do; action discretization does not), and either quote the supporting result in [18] or re-anchor the claim.

6. **"Model-agnostic" is claimed four times and supported by a second policy that appears in no table**: the phrase appears in the Abstract, in §I's first contribution bullet, in §III's opening, and as "agnostic to the policy architecture" in §III-A. The evidence base is two policies, two tasks, one embodiment (WidowX), one fixed third-person camera, and one simulator. GR00T appears only inside Fig. 3, whose caption reports no values; Tables I, II and III are pi0-only, and §IV-D states outright that pi0 was chosen as the base policy. A reader therefore cannot check a single GR00T number. That leaves the main result — the distractor-density sweep, the paper's central experiment — reported graphically with no numeric table at all. With one policy tabulated, the evaluation cannot distinguish policy-agnosticism from pi0-specific behavior, and "Demonstrated Clutter Robustness at Scale" describes episode count rather than task or embodiment diversity.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §IV Results — expected a numeric table of GR00T success rates supporting the model-agnostic claim; checked Table I, Table II, Table III, §IV-B, Fig. 3 caption`
   **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: I evaluate open VLA policies on exactly these benchmarks and verified the table contents directly
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — add a numeric table for Fig. 3 (both policies, both tasks, both clutter types) so GR00T's results are inspectable; the ICRA +2-page allowance accommodates this. Then either (a) retain "model-agnostic" as an architectural property claim explicitly scoped to two policies, or (b) soften to "policy-independent by construction, verified on two policies". **Strengthening / future work**: a third architecture (OpenVLA [1] or Octo [3]) would convert the claim from asserted to demonstrated.

7. **The Abstract and Conclusion generalize past the paper's own conditional finding**: §IV-B reports that CGVD "consistently underperforms the baseline" on Carrot-on-Plate and explains why. The Abstract nonetheless states that CGVD "prevents performance collapse" without qualification, and §VI repeats it. More sharply, both the Abstract and §VI describe visual distillation as a "prerequisite" for robust manipulation — a prerequisite is a necessary condition, and the paper's own carrot-on-plate result demonstrates that it is not one. Compounding this, the negative result is never quantified: no table reports carrot-on-plate, so the reader cannot size the deficit, and §V's description of it as a "slight degradation" sits uneasily against §IV-B's "consistently underperforms". The honest finding here is stronger than the overclaim: CGVD is a *distractor-type-conditional* intervention that helps against semantically confusable clutter and hurts when clutter is contextually informative. That is a more credible, more citable, and more useful result, and it makes the paper's own semantic/random/attribute taxonomy the scientific contribution.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-B "it consistently underperforms the baseline in this specific scenario"`
   **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: the internal contradiction is verifiable from the manuscript alone
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — reframe the Abstract and §VI conditionally ("under semantic and attribute clutter"), replace "prerequisite" with a claim the evidence supports, and reconcile §V's "slight" with §IV-B's "consistently". **Strengthening**: add the carrot-on-plate numbers to a table so the boundary condition is quantified rather than described.

8. **§VI claims OOD-target generalization that no experiment tests**: the Conclusion states that CGVD "improves generalization to out-of-distribution targets". §IV contains no such experiment. Both tasks are in-distribution Bridge tasks with in-distribution targets (spoon, carrot); the RoboCasa [29] and YCB [32] objects are *distractors*, not targets, and no condition varies target identity away from the training distribution. §IV-C tests compositional attribute prompts, which is prompt-structure generalization, not out-of-distribution targets. Either this is drafting residue from an earlier version, or it is an intended claim requiring an experiment that does not exist. Left in place, it is the kind of sentence downstream papers cite.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §IV Experiments — expected an out-of-distribution target experiment supporting the §VI generalization claim; checked §IV-A object sources, §IV-B, §IV-C, Table I, Table II`
   **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: I work with Bridge and Open X-Embodiment directly and confirmed both task targets are in-distribution
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — delete the clause, or add an OOD-target condition (novel target categories not in the Bridge distribution) and report it. Deletion is entirely acceptable; the paper loses nothing it demonstrated.

9. **"Architecturally excluded" overstates the guarantee, and the metric that would prove it is never reported**: §I claims the target is "architecturally excluded" from the distractor mask, and §I criticizes BYOVLA [9] for providing "only probabilistic protection". But CGVD's protection is only as reliable as SAM3's safe-set segmentation plus the two-layer heuristic — the set-theoretic subtraction in Eq. (5) guarantees nothing about objects SAM3 fails to place in the safe set. The paper concedes this itself: §IV-D reports that removing the refinement layer causes "genuine targets to be erroneously inpainted out of the scene". So CGVD can also delete the target; the difference from [9] is the failure rate, not the failure mode. The one number that would substantiate the differentiator — the safe-set survival or target-deletion rate across episodes — is never reported, even though it is trivially measurable in simulation and would be far more diagnostic than the success rates alone.
   **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §III-E and §IV — expected a measured target-preservation or target-deletion rate substantiating architectural exclusion; checked §III-D, §III-E, Table II ablation, §V`
   **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: this is the standard failure-mode audit for segmentation-gated pipelines
   **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — either report target-preservation rate (with and without the refinement layers, and ideally against [9]'s protection rate), or replace "architecturally excluded" with an accurate description of the guarantee, e.g. that the target is excluded conditional on correct safe-set segmentation.

10. **§II does not instantiate §I's taxonomy, two headings are near-duplicates with swapped content, and the closest prior work is never discussed**: §I promises three categories — adaptation, inference-time intervention, training-time augmentation. §II delivers: II-A (VLA models), II-B (vision foundation models — a fourth topic, and the paper's own tooling rather than a robustness approach), II-C (domain randomization and pre-training, plus adaptation), II-D (inference-time intervention). The training-time augmentation family thus has no subsection at all. Meanwhile §II-B is titled "Robustness via Adaptation and Data Scaling" while its content is entirely VFMs, and §II-C is titled "Robustness via Adaptation and Domain Randomization" while covering the adaptation-and-scaling material §II-B's title promises — the two titles are near-identical and the content is transposed. The substantive cost is that [12] (NICE scene surgery) — pixel-space scene intervention, the nearest neighbour to CGVD's core operation, and from the same group as [6], on which the paper's motivating premise rests — receives one clause in §I and no discussion anywhere. Also worth clarifying: the semantic/random/attribute distractor taxonomy in §IV-A closely tracks the framing in [6]; if it is adopted from there, it should be credited.
    **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §II-B and §II-C headings "Robustness via Adaptation and Data Scaling" and "Robustness via Adaptation and Domain Randomization"`
    **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: literature structure in this subfield, verified against the manuscript's own §I taxonomy
    **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — retitle §II-B to "Vision Foundation Models for Robotic Perception" (or similar) and §II-C to match its content, and add a paragraph positioning CGVD against [10]-[12], especially [12], stating what pixel-space scene surgery at training time does and does not give you at deployment. 11. **The sim-to-real bounding argument is undercut by the paper's own §III-G, and §V omits the scope limit entirely**: §IV-A argues that because SAM3 [13] and LaMa [14] were trained on real imagery, "sim-to-real risk is bounded to policy-level transfer". Two problems. First, §III-G states that robot mask protection uses "SimplerEnv's ground-truth robot mask boundary" and that SAM3 "can achieve the similar proprioceptive protection" on hardware — asserted, never tested, and Table II shows this component is worth 4.5 points (77.5 → 73.0), so a noisier real-world mask directly degrades the reported number. Second, the argument is partly backwards: applying real-image-trained SAM3 and LaMa to *rendered* SimplerEnv observations is itself an out-of-distribution application whose behavior need not predict real-image behavior in either direction. Compounding this, §V lists three limitations — static background, inpainting fidelity, startup latency — and never states that no real-robot experiment was performed, while §VI claims a prerequisite for "deploying foundation models in unstructured manipulation tasks". The most consequential scope limit is absent from the section whose job is to state it.
    **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-A "ensuring that the sim-to-real risk is bounded to policy-level transfer, which SimplerEnv [31] explicitly addresses"`
    **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise on SimplerEnv and perception-model domain shift; genuine uncertainty about how closely SAM3 robot masks would match ground truth on hardware, which is precisely why it needs measuring
    **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — add simulation-only evaluation to §V as an explicit limitation, and soften §IV-A's bounding argument to acknowledge that rendered-image perception is itself a domain shift. **Strengthening / future work**: even a single real-robot episode set with SAM3-derived robot masks, or a sim ablation substituting a SAM3 mask for the ground-truth mask, would convert the assertion into evidence cheaply.

The text's claim is not supportable from the numbers directly above it. This matters because efficiency is one axis on which the paper distinguishes itself from [9]'s "multiple VLA forward passes per region": a 33% per-step cost plus 4.9 s of startup is a defensible trade, but it needs stating as a trade rather than as no cost.

14. **Policy checkpoints are never identified, so the numbers cannot be situated against published SimplerEnv results**: §IV-A names pi0 and GR00T but not which checkpoints, whether Bridge-finetuned, or which SimplerEnv Bridge configuration. Reported 0-distractor success rates (86.0% simple, 85.0% complex) cannot be compared against published SimplerEnv baselines without this, so a reader cannot tell whether the setup is standard or whether the baseline is unusually strong or weak. The *relative* deltas survive this — seeds are matched — so this affects positioning rather than the core result.
    **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §IV-A Experimental Setup — expected the specific pi0 and GR00T checkpoints and their SimplerEnv Bridge configuration; checked §IV-A, §IV-B, §IV-D, Table I caption`
    **Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: I run these checkpoints on this benchmark
    **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** (two sentences) — name the checkpoints and cite the published SimplerEnv success rate for the same task so readers can locate your baseline.

15. **Eq. (3) is undefined when the IoU-filtered distractor set is empty**: g(s_i) takes a max over {d_j : IoU(s_i, d_j) > η}. When no distractor overlaps s_i above η — the common case for an isolated target — the maximum is over the empty set and the expression has no value. Presumably the intent is 0 (yielding g = σ_safe), but the paper does not say, and the choice propagates into Eq. (4)'s composite score.
    **Evidence Anchor**: `equation: Eq. (3) — the max over the IoU-filtered distractor set is undefined when no distractor satisfies IoU > η`
    **Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: formal reading of the stated definition
    **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** (one clause) — define the empty-set convention, and report η and the dilation radii r_d, r_s, r_e, which are also unspecified.

16. **§IV-C conflates two of the three distractor types the paper itself defines**: §IV-A defines Semantic, Random and Attribute as three distinct types, but §IV-C describes the Table I condition as "0–4 random attribute distractors", while Table I's caption calls them "attribute distractors". Since the paper's clearest scientific contribution is that the effect is distractor-type-dependent, the type labels need to be used consistently or the finding becomes unreadable.
    **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-C "we evaluate the baseline and CGVD across 0–4 random attribute distractors using two prompt structures"`
    **Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: internal inconsistency verified against §IV-A's own definitions
    **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** (wording) — state which of the three defined types Table I uses, and if it is a mixture, say so and explain why.

17. **The "adversarial patch" explanation in §IV-D is a strong mechanistic claim with no support**: §IV-D explains the mean-color-fill result by asserting that "the stark, unnatural region boundaries act as adversarial patches to the VLA's ViT backbone, directly disrupting planning". Adversarial patches are optimized against a specific model; a flat-colour region is an out-of-distribution texture, which is a different phenomenon with different implications. No citation and no measurement supports the claim, and the paper's own §IV-B offers a competing explanation for inpainting effects (generative artifacts disrupting spatial geometry).
    **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-D "the stark, unnatural region boundaries act as adversarial patches to the VLA's ViT backbone, directly disrupting planning"`
    **Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise on ViT robustness; the alternative explanation is plausible but I have not measured it either
    **Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** (wording) — soften to a hypothesis, or cite the adversarial-patch literature and say what would distinguish it from a plain out-of-distribution-texture account.

18. **The Abstract claims geometry preservation that §IV-B contradicts**: the Abstract says the distilled observation preserves "critical spatial geometry", but inpainting replaces the masked region with hallucinated background rather than preserving what was there, and §IV-B concedes that "aggressive inpainting... can occasionally introduce generative artifacts that disrupt the spatial geometry". A related, unstated consequence: because Eq.     **Suggestion**: **Strengthening** — qualify the Abstract ("preserving background context and scene layout outside masked regions"), and add the t=0 robot-occlusion consequence to §V.

### Detailed Comments

#### Literature Review

- **Coverage**: For the VLA and VFM axes, coverage is current and appropriate — RT-2 [2], OpenVLA [1], Octo [3], pi0 [5], GR00T [4], SAM 3 [13], GroundingDINO [19], and the 2025-2026 clutter-robustness work [6], [7], [8], [30] are all present. Coverage is thin in three places. (i) The training-time augmentation family [10]-[12] is cited once and never engaged, despite [12] being the nearest pixel-space neighbour. (ii) There is no attention-quantification literature at all, which is why §II's mechanism claims have no methodological path to verification. (iii) The "add information via visual prompting" paradigm that §II-B and §II-D contrast against is represented by a single reference [23], which is thin for a paradigm the paper defines itself against — mark-based visual prompting has a well-known line of work.
- **Integration quality**: §II-A and §II-D read as genuine critical synthesis: §II-D in particular does the right thing by locating DTP [30] in feature space and CGVD in pixel space, and by naming a specific mechanism for why feature-space pruning should struggle on semantically similar distractors. §II-B and §II-C are weaker — §II-B is largely enumerative (VFMs have been used for value maps [20], semantic maps [21], labelling [22]) before pivoting to the paper's inversion claim, and §II-C compresses domain randomization, internet-scale pre-training, and adaptation into one paragraph without distinguishing their failure modes. The heading transposition (W10) suggests §II was restructured late and the titles were not updated.
- **Research gap argument**: The gap is stated clearly and is real — inference-time, training-free, deterministic protection of the target is a genuine hole between [8]'s retraining cost and [9]'s probabilistic protection. The argument's weakness is that it is entirely *theoretical*: every competitor is defeated in prose, none in an experiment (W1), and CGVD's own claimed determinism is not measured (W9). A gap argued by asserted competitor weaknesses is less persuasive than one closed by a table.

#### Theoretical Framework

- **Appropriateness**: The concept-gating framework — instruction defines a safe set, everything else is a removal candidate, set-theoretic subtraction protects the safe set — is a good fit for the problem and is genuinely simpler than the alternatives. Framing distractor suppression as a *set operation* rather than a *confidence threshold* is the right instinct and is the paper's clearest conceptual contribution.
- **Application depth**: Mixed. The framework is applied properly where it is specified: Eq. (5)'s asymmetric dilation (r_s ≥ r_d) is a thoughtful realization of the protective-buffer idea, and Eqs. (3)-(4) do real work rather than decorating the pipeline. But the framework's central claim — "the instruction determines the gate" — is only half implemented, because D is not derived from the instruction and its provenance is unstated (W2). And the information-bottleneck framing (W4) is named rather than applied: no rate–distortion trade-off, no learned encoder, no information-theoretic quantity is computed anywhere.
- **Alternative frameworks**: Two would fit the paper's own evidence better than the information bottleneck. (i) **Causal confusion in imitation learning** (de Haan et al., 2019) is almost exactly the phenomenon: a policy latching onto nuisance visual variables that correlate with, but do not cause, correct behavior. It explains both the positive semantic-clutter result *and* the negative carrot-on-plate result, where contextual clutter is genuinely informative rather than nuisance — a single framework covering both halves of the paper's finding. (ii) **Object-centric / entity-factored representation** framings would motivate why removing rather than reweighting is the right intervention. Either would give §IV-B's conditional result a principled account instead of a post-hoc one.

#### Academic Argument Quality

- **Factual accuracy**: Three specific issues. Action discretization is named as a partial cause of the failure mode, but neither evaluated policy discretizes actions (W5). "High-pass filter" describes the opposite of the behavior claimed (W4). "Negligible overhead" and "maintaining the VLA's native control frequency" are contradicted by Table III and by the table's own caption (W12). One item to verify rather than a stated error: [18]'s use in §II-A and §II-D appears to run against that paper's headline robustness findings; please quote the supporting result.
- **Argument logic**: The dominant pattern is claim-inflation between sections: a conditional result in §IV-B becomes unconditional in the Abstract and §VI (W7); a comparative claim in the Abstract has no experiment in §IV (W1); a mechanism illustrated in one figure becomes a named phenomenon across four sections (W3); an unevaluated OOD claim appears in §VI (W8). Individually each is repairable by editing; collectively they mean the paper's rhetorical register runs consistently ahead of its evidence. The word "prerequisite" is the sharpest instance, since a prerequisite is a necessary condition and §IV-B supplies the counterexample.
- **Terminology precision**: "High-frequency" carries two incompatible senses (W4). The three distractor types are defined precisely in §IV-A and then blended in §IV-C (W16). "Adversarial patch" is used for what is more likely an out-of-distribution texture (W17). "Visual proprioception" is defensible usage but should be disambiguated once from state proprioception, which pi0 and GR00T also consume as input. "Architecturally excluded" claims a guarantee the construction does not provide (W9).

#### Contribution to the Field

- **Incremental contribution**: Real and worth publishing, but smaller than the paper claims. The pipeline shape — parse instruction, segment, remove distractors, run frozen policy — is BYOVLA's [9]. The genuine deltas are (i) replacing the VLM-plus-sensitivity-probe with a deterministic parse plus the cross-validation genuineness score, which is cheaper and removes an external API dependency, and (ii) the empirical observation that the intervention's sign depends on distractor type. The second is arguably the more valuable contribution and is currently buried in §IV-B.
- **Positioning**: Prior work is described accurately at the level of mechanism, and §II-D's feature-space-vs-pixel-space distinction is a fair and useful framing. What is missing is *empirical* positioning: the paper distinguishes itself from four named methods and measures itself against none of them (W1), and the nearest pixel-space prior [12] is not discussed (W10). For a system whose novelty is a re-engineering of an existing pipeline, the head-to-head is not optional garnish — it is the experiment that sizes the contribution.
- **Overclaiming**: This is the paper's central problem, and it is fixable without any new experiments in most instances. "Significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines" (Abstract) needs a comparison or removal. "Prevents policy collapse" and "critical prerequisite" (Abstract, §VI) need conditioning on distractor type. "Improves generalization to out-of-distribution targets" (§VI) needs an experiment or deletion. "Model-agnostic" (four occurrences) needs scoping to the evidence. "Demonstrated Clutter Robustness at Scale" (§I) describes episode count, not task or embodiment breadth. A version of this paper that claimed exactly what it showed — a training-free wrapper that substantially improves pi0 under semantic and attribute clutter on Bridge tasks, degrades it under contextually informative clutter, and is cheaper than VLM-based alternatives — would be a stronger ICRA submission than the current one, and would be easier to defend in rebuttal.

### Questions for Authors

1. **How is the distractor set D populated at inference time?** Is it a fixed generic vocabulary, derived per-episode from some source, or the ground-truth list of injected RoboCasa/YCB categories? If the last, how should a reader interpret the 77.5 vs 43.0 comparison, given the baseline has no equivalent scene knowledge? This is the single question whose answer most affects my assessment.

2. **Why is there no comparison against BYOVLA [9]?** It is the same intervention family, ICRA-published, and the method §I critiques most directly. If [30] and [8] lack public implementations, please state that. If a BYOVLA comparison was attempted and something went wrong, that is also useful information for the rebuttal.

3. **For Fig. 4: which policy, which layer, which heads, what aggregation method, and how many episodes?** And is there any quantitative version — attention mass on target vs. distractor regions, aggregated across episodes — that could be reported instead of, or alongside, the qualitative panels?

4. **What is the target-preservation rate?** How often across your 19,200 episodes does the safe set survive distillation intact, with and without the two-layer refinement? This is measurable in simulation and would directly substantiate the "architecturally excluded" differentiator over [9]'s "probabilistic protection".

5. **What are the carrot-on-plate numbers?** §IV-B says CGVD "consistently underperforms" while §V calls it a "slight degradation"; a table would let readers judge the boundary condition and would strengthen rather than weaken the paper.

6. **Which pi0 and GR00T checkpoints were used, and what does §VI's "improves generalization to out-of-distribution targets" refer to?** If the latter is drafting residue, saying so is a complete answer.
