### Summary Assessment

CGVD is a training-free, inference-time perception wrapper that parses a manipulation instruction into a safe set and a distractor set, segments both with SAM3, subtracts the safe mask from the distractor mask, inpaints the residue with LaMa, and hands the distilled frame to a frozen VLA. It is evaluated on two SimplerEnv Bridge tasks with two policies (pi0, GR00T) at up to 18 injected distractors, with a 200-episode, 10-seed, matched-seed protocol. The topic is squarely in ICRA's Learning for Manipulation scope, the engineering is sensible, and the evaluation protocol is above the median for this paper class.

The problem is altitude and claim discipline, not fit. The Abstract asserts CGVD "significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines," but the only comparator anywhere in §IV is the un-augmented policy — neither BYOVLA [9], an ICRA paper the authors argue against on three separate grounds, nor DTP [30] is run. The headline 77.5 vs 43.0 is one cell of the ablation table (pi0, spoon-on-towel, 18 semantic distractors), while §IV-B honestly reports CGVD consistently losing on carrot-on-plate — a result the Abstract and Conclusion do not reflect. §IV-E calls the overhead "negligible" where Table III's own caption says "moderate" and the numbers show +32.8% per step. The Conclusion claims OOD-target generalization that no experiment addresses.

I credit the authors' willingness to publish their negative result; the integrity is real. The framing has simply not caught up with it. Reframe the contribution as integration-and-evaluation, run one intervention baseline, and this becomes a credible ICRA paper.

### Strengths

1. **Honest reporting of a negative result**: §IV-B states plainly that CGVD underperforms the baseline on carrot-on-plate and offers a mechanistic explanation (contextual clutter acts as a useful visual anchor); Table I likewise reports CGVD losing at 1 distractor on both prompt structures. Papers in this class routinely bury such results. This is exactly the reporting behaviour the ICRA reviewer pool should reward, and I want it preserved rather than edited away in revision. **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-B — "it consistently underperforms the baseline in this specific scenario"`
2. **Architectural rather than probabilistic target protection is a genuine design idea**: Eq. (5)'s asymmetric dilation, with r_s ≥ r_d, means the target cannot be inpainted out by construction rather than by a threshold surviving. This is a real and checkable distinction from sensitivity-probe approaches, and it is the most defensible piece of the novelty argument. **Evidence Anchor**: `equation: Eq. (5) — the safe-set dilation radius condition r_s ≥ r_d, which creates a protective buffer by construction`
3. **Matched-seed evaluation protocol**: Pairing baseline and treatment on identical seeds within each condition removes a major nuisance-variance source and is a deliberate design choice rather than a default. Combined with 200 episodes per condition across 10 seeds, this is a more careful protocol than most inference-wrapper submissions bring to ICRA. **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-A — "totaling 200 episodes per task/distractor count, with matched seeds between baseline and CGVD"`
4. **Two-architecture evaluation supports the model-agnostic claim**: Running both pi0 and GR00T with and without CGVD across two tasks and two distractor types is the right experimental shape for a claim of policy-architecture independence; single-policy evaluations cannot support that claim at all. **Evidence Anchor**: `figure: Fig. 3 — success-rate curves for both pi0 and GR00T with and without CGVD, across two tasks and two distractor types`
5. **Latency is measured and reported at all**: Many inference-time-intervention papers report no timing whatsoever. Disclosing a 4,914 ms initialization cost is an act of transparency that works against the authors' own narrative, and I credit the measurement even while disputing the word "negligible" attached to it. **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table III — Initialization (t=0) row reporting 4,914 ms, a cost most inference-wrapper papers omit entirely`
6. **Clean articulation of the contribution's practical shape**: The wrapper framing is stated precisely and early, so a reader knows within one paragraph exactly what integration cost the method implies. For an ICRA practitioner audience deciding whether to adopt, this clarity is worth a lot. **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §III — "CGVD operates as a perception wrapper around any Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, requiring no fine-tuning or policy modification"`

### Detailed Comments

#### Journal Fit

Scope fit is unambiguous and is not at issue. A training-free, model-agnostic inference-time perception wrapper for frozen VLA policies, evaluated on tabletop manipulation with pi0 and GR00T, belongs in the Deep Learning in Robotics and Automation / Learning for Manipulation area. The readership is the right one, the topic is timely, and the practitioner value of a no-retraining intervention is real.

What is at issue is whether this is a *robotics* contribution or a CV preprocessing step evaluated on a robot benchmark. Right now the paper is closer to the latter than the authors intend, for a specific and fixable reason: everything robot-specific in the pipeline — the ground-truth robot mask, the injected-and-therefore-known distractor inventory, the fixed third-person camera, the cached static background — is either simulator-supplied or assumed away. Strip those, and what remains is segment-subtract-inpaint. The path back to a robotics contribution runs through W3: show that the robot-mask protection survives replacement by a predicted mask, and state how D is obtained when nobody injected the clutter. That is the single highest-leverage revision in this review after W1.

On framing, my direct advice: adopt integration-and-evaluation. The paper is a good systems-and-benchmark contribution — matched-seed protocol, two architectures, a clutter-density sweep, an honest negative result — and a weak mechanism-novelty contribution. Claiming the second invites reviewers to test Eqs. (3)-(4) against the ICRA novelty bar, where they will not clear it. Claiming the first invites reviewers to test the evaluation, which is where this paper is strong, once the comparator gap is closed.

**Page budget.** At 7 pages against 6+2, there is roughly one page of headroom and the required additions need essentially all of it. Recoverable space, in priority order: (i) §II-C, which duplicates §I's fourth paragraph — merge and delete, roughly a quarter column; (ii) §II-B's VFM survey, which overlaps §I's fifth paragraph — compress to three sentences, roughly a fifth of a column; (iii) §III-A, which restates the introduction's problem statement in notation — compress to three sentences, roughly a sixth of a column; (iv) Fig. 1, the qualitative teaser, which carries the same message as Fig. 4 — drop it or render it single-column, roughly a third of a column. Total recovered is on the order of a full column-page. That funds a numeric main-results table (~0.4 column), one baseline-comparison table or row block (~0.3 column), a hyperparameter table (~0.15 column), and the Fig. 4 method sentences — with the 8th page held in reserve at fee rather than spent on redundancy.

**Consolidated required set for ICRA acceptance**: W1 (comparator or full claim reframe), W3's in-simulation SAM3-mask substitution plus an explicit statement of how D is built, W4 (numeric results with dispersion), W5 (latency harmonization), W6 (delete or support the OOD claim), W7 (cite and explain Fig. 4, or drop the mechanism claim), W8's hyperparameter table and availability statement, W9 (qualify the headline; surface the regression in Abstract and Conclusion), W10, W11's heading fix, and the Minor Issues below. **Strengthening / future work**: a real-robot demonstration, Layer 1 vs Layer 2 ablation, an η/r_d/r_s sensitivity sweep, full code release, and dynamic-clutter handling. The required set is feasible within the page budget and within one cycle without new hardware.

#### Originality

The originality claim has two components with very different footing. The architectural target-protection idea — Eq. (5)'s asymmetric dilation making the target un-inpaintable by construction rather than by threshold survival — is a genuine and clearly stated distinction from probabilistic approaches, and it is the piece I would build the novelty case on. The Interaction-Aware Masking Logic of Eqs. (3)-(4), which §I nominates as the contribution, is a confidence-differential heuristic with an unjustified functional form and no component-level validation.

The novelty *delta* is the deeper problem. §I and §II-D both argue against [9] and [30] on the merits, at some length, and §IV runs neither. Three asserted advantages over [9] — no external API, no per-region sensitivity probe, architectural rather than probabilistic protection — are all testable and none is tested. Worse, the first of them is contingent on the closed distractor vocabulary discussed in W3: [9] needs a VLM precisely because it does not know the distractor inventory in advance, so "no additional API" is not obviously a free improvement but a different point on a coverage-versus-cost trade-off that the paper does not acknowledge. I would find a measured comparison persuasive even if CGVD lost on some axis; I do not find the argument persuasive without one.

A secondary note on positioning: [6], [8], [12], and [30] — four of the works the novelty argument leans on hardest — are unrefereed preprints. ICRA reviewers accept preprint citations routinely, but a differentiation argument resting mostly on unrefereed work is structurally weaker than one resting on [9], which is refereed, at this venue, and unrun.

#### Significance

If the deployability questions resolve favourably, the significance for this readership is high: a wrapper that improves clutter robustness in frozen commercial-scale VLAs with no retraining is directly actionable for a large fraction of ICRA manipulation practitioners. The clutter-density sweep to 18 distractors is genuinely informative and is the kind of systematic characterization the field is short on.

Two things currently bound the significance. First, the +32.8% per-step latency and ~2.4 Hz resulting rate (W5) narrow the set of tasks where this is adoptable, and the paper's own "negligible" framing obscures rather than addresses that. Second, the static-background assumption in §V is a real constraint honestly stated — but combined with the cached single-inpaint design, it confines the method to scenes where nothing but the robot and target move. Both are legitimate scope limits; the paper should own them in the Abstract rather than only in §V, because the Abstract currently implies a broader operating envelope than §V concedes.

The carrot-on-plate result also carries more significance than the paper gives it. "Aggressive masking can deprive a VLA of useful contextual anchors" is a finding the community should know, and it argues for a gating criterion on *when* to distill. That is arguably more interesting than the headline, and it is currently buried in one paragraph of §IV-B.

#### Structural Coherence

Title → Abstract → §I → §III → §IV is coherent. §IV → Abstract and §IV → §VI are not, in three specific places: the SOTA-superiority claim (W1), the negligible-latency claim (W5), and the OOD-generalization claim (W6). This is a textbook over-promise/under-deliver pattern, and it is all the more frustrating because the body text is candid — §IV-B and §V both state limitations the front and back matter then quietly drop. The fix is to propagate §IV's honesty upward, not to soften §IV.

The Related Work structure additionally does not track §I's own three-category taxonomy (W11), and training-time augmentation [10, 11, 12] receives a §I category with no §II subsection. Figure placement compounds this: Fig. 3, the main-results figure, floats into §II ahead of §II-A, roughly two pages from the §IV-B text that discusses it, and Figs. 1, 2, and 4 are not cited from the body at all.

#### Title & Abstract

The title is accurate, descriptive, and appropriately keyword-dense for ICRA search and bidding. "Overcoming" slightly overstates given the carrot-on-plate regression; "Mitigating" would be defensible and would cost nothing.

The Abstract is the weakest surface in the manuscript relative to its influence. It carries the unsupported SOTA-superiority claim (W1), presents an ablation-table cell as the general result without naming its condition (W9), omits the regression entirely, and closes with the ungrammatical "in the clutter." Three of the four are one-sentence fixes. Given that most ICRA reviewers form their initial disposition from the Abstract, this is the highest return-per-word revision in the paper.

#### Conclusion

§VI restates the contribution accurately in its first two sentences, then adds the unevaluated OOD-generalization claim (W6) and characterizes the approach as "highly efficient" — a word the +32.8% execution overhead and 4,914 ms startup do not obviously support, and which sits oddly beside §V's own admission of a startup latency. The static-background limitation is properly carried forward from §V, which is good practice. The future-work direction (real-time mask updating for interactive clutter) is well chosen and follows directly from the stated limitation.

The Conclusion should also acknowledge the carrot-on-plate result. A paper that reports a negative finding in §IV and then omits it from both Abstract and Conclusion invites the reading that the omission was strategic — which, given how forthcoming §IV-B is, would be an unfair reading the authors can easily foreclose.

### Questions for Authors

1. How is the distractor set D constructed at deployment time, when nobody has injected the clutter and the inventory is unknown? Was D in these experiments enumerated from the SimplerEnv or RoboCasa/YCB asset list used for injection? If so, how does the "no additional API" advantage over [9] survive in an open-world setting where the inventory must itself be proposed?
2. Can you report the Table II robot-mask-protection condition using a SAM3-predicted robot mask in place of SimplerEnv's ground-truth mask, in simulation? This is the single cheapest experiment that would test §IV-A's "sim-to-real risk is bounded to policy-level transfer" argument against the §III-G component that most obviously violates it. What is the success-rate delta?
3. Is a comparison against BYOVLA [9] or DTP [30] feasible before the rebuttal deadline, even reimplemented, even at reduced episode counts, and even on only the 18-semantic-distractor spoon-on-towel condition? If not, are the authors willing to remove all superiority-over-SOTA language and reframe the contribution as integration-and-evaluation?
4. What are the success rates at 18 semantic distractors for GR00T, and for carrot-on-plate under both policies? Since 77.5 and 43.0 come from one cell of Table II, the surrounding cells determine whether the headline is representative or exceptional.
5. For Fig. 4: how were the attention maps extracted — which policy, which layer or layers, and what head aggregation? And is Fig. 4 intended to be cited from §IV, where it is currently absent?
