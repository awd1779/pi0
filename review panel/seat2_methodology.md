### Summary Assessment

CGVD is a training-free perception wrapper that parses an instruction into safe and distractor concept sets, segments both with SAM3, subtracts them set-theoretically, and inpaints the residue with LaMa before the observation reaches a VLA policy. It is evaluated in SimplerEnv over roughly 23,800 rollouts across two tasks, two architectures, three distractor types, and an ablation and latency benchmark. The design is genuinely good: distractor count is manipulated cleanly, seeds are matched across arms, and adverse results are reported rather than hidden. The headline effect is also real — I recomputed 77.5 vs 43.0 as Δ = 34.5 pp with an interval of about ±9.0 pp, which survives even a conservatively seed-clustered widening.

The gap is between that design and its analysis. Two captions claim "statistical significance" while the paper contains no interval, dispersion, or test of any kind; the matched-seed structure that would give the authors more power is never used; roughly 62 baseline-versus-CGVD contrasts run uncorrected. On my recomputation, 6 of Table I's 10 deltas and one ablation row are consistent with zero. Separately, two possible privileged-information channels — the underived distractor vocabulary D and SimplerEnv's ground-truth robot mask — are never addressed. Most of this is repairable from data already collected, which is why I recommend Major Revision rather than rejection.

### Strengths

1. **Evaluation scale and factorial structure are well above the norm for this claim class**: distractor count is manipulated as a continuous dose across two tasks, two architectures, and two distractor types in a fully crossed grid, at 200 rollouts per cell. This is the right shape of experiment for a robustness claim, and it is what makes the recommended re-analysis cheap: the data to answer nearly every statistical objection below already exist. **Evidence Anchor**: `text: Fig. 3 caption "totaling 19,200 episodes for the results visualized in this figure"`
2. **Seed-matched allocation between arms is the correct design choice for stochastic rollouts**: matching seeds removes the dominant nuisance variance (initial-state and distractor-placement draws) from the comparison, which is exactly what a paired design should do. This is a deliberate, non-obvious methodological decision and it deserves credit independently of the fact that the analysis does not yet exploit it. **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-A "with matched seeds between baseline and CGVD"`
3. **The headline effect is large enough to survive honest interval estimation**: at n = 200 per arm, Δ = 34.5 pp yields a 95% interval of roughly [25.5, 43.5] pp treating arms as independent, and roughly [19.2, 49.8] pp after inflating by a design effect corresponding to a seed ICC of 0.10. The central claim of the paper is not at risk from the reporting gaps below, and that materially shapes my recommendation. **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table II — Baseline (no CGVD) 43.0 vs CGVD (full pipeline) 77.5, Δ = 34.5 pp`
4. **Adverse results are reported rather than suppressed**: the carrot-on-plate regression is stated in §IV-B, repeated in §V, and mechanistically discussed; Table I retains two negative deltas rather than dropping them. Reporting a subgroup that reverses the aggregate direction is precisely what guards a benchmark paper against a Simpson-style misreading, and the authors did it voluntarily. **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §V "aggressive inpainting of clutter can inadvertently lead to a slight degradation in task success rates compared to the baseline"`
5. **The ablation covers all three structural components against a common comparator**: each of the pipeline's claimed contributions (inpainting quality, two-layer refinement, robot-mask protection) is varied against the same full-pipeline reference at a fixed condition, and the resulting ordering is informative about which component carries the effect. **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table II — rows "– Mean-color fill" 56.5, "– Two-layer target refinement" 65.0, "– Robot mask protection" 73.0`
6. **Cost is designed for, and measured, rather than assumed**: confining SAM3 and LaMa to t = 0 and caching the clean plate is the right architectural decision for a control loop, and the authors instrument it and publish both phases separately instead of quoting a single averaged figure. The interpretation overstates the result (W9), but the measurement structure itself is sound. **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table III — Initialization (t=0) 4,914 ms vs Execution (t>0) 317 → 421 ms`

### Weaknesses

1. **"Statistical significance" is asserted with no inferential statistic anywhere in the paper**: two captions state that a sample size was chosen "to ensure statistical significance," and results prose uses "significantly" at least twice, yet the manuscript contains no confidence interval, standard deviation, standard error, error bar, per-seed spread, test statistic, or *p*-value. Sample size alone does not establish significance. Recomputing 95% intervals on the differences of two independent binomial proportions at n = 200 per arm (see Detailed Comments → Analysis Methods for the full table), 6 of Table I's 10 deltas contain zero — including +4.0 at 0 distractors ([−2.4, +10.4]), −2.0 at 1 ([−10.0, +6.0]), +7.0 at 3 ([−1.8, +15.8]), and +2.0 complex/0 ([−4.8, +8.8]). In Table II, the robot-mask row's −4.5 pp yields [−12.9, +3.9] and is likewise consistent with zero. *Why it matters*: as reported, a reader cannot separate the four deltas that are real from the six that are indistinguishable from noise, and the paper narrates them uniformly. *Suggestion* (**required for ICRA acceptance**, near-zero page cost): either report per-seed spread and an interval alongside every headline number and delete the two "to ensure statistical significance" clauses, or explicitly label the per-cell readings as descriptive. — **Severity**: Major | **Evidence Anchor**: `text: Fig. 3 caption "To ensure statistical significance, each data point represents the average success rate over 200 independent evaluation rollouts"` | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: interval estimation over episode-level Bernoulli outcomes
2. **The matched-seed paired design is created but never exploited, and episodes are treated as independent despite clustering**: §IV-A establishes matched seeds, which licenses a seed-level paired analysis with substantially more power than the unpaired bound I was forced to use, yet only marginal means appear. Separately, 20 episodes share each seed, so the 200 rollouts are not "independent": with a modest ICC of 0.05, the design effect is 1 + 19(0.05) = 1.95, effective n falls to about 103, and every interval widens by roughly 40%. Applying that inflation, only 3 of Table I's 10 deltas retain intervals excluding zero. *Why it matters*: the current reporting is simultaneously less powerful than the design allows (paired structure discarded) and more optimistic than the data support (clustering ignored) — an unusual combination that a seed-level analysis fixes in both directions at once. *Suggestion* (**required for ICRA acceptance**): report per-seed deltas and a paired analysis (Wilcoxon signed-rank over 10 seed pairs, or a mixed-effects logistic model with a random intercept for seed and cluster-robust intervals); the latter is the stronger option and costs no new rollouts. Also report the observed ICC so readers can judge the clustering directly. — **Severity**: Major | **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §IV-B Main Results — expected a seed-level paired analysis or clustered interval exploiting the matched seeds; checked §IV-A, §IV-B, §IV-C, §IV-D, Tables I–III` | **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: seed-matched paired designs and cluster-robust estimation
3. **The distractor set D has no stated derivation, leaving open that CGVD is scored with oracle knowledge of the injected perturbation**: §III-B introduces D = {d_1, …, d_K} with the example "spatula, fork, knife" but never says where the list comes from. It cannot come from the instruction — the instruction never mentions distractors — and §III-C requires each d_k as an explicit SAM3 text prompt, so D must be an external, finite, hand-specified vocabulary. §IV-A meanwhile injects distractors drawn from RoboCasa [29] and YCB [32]. If D was populated from that same injection list, CGVD is evaluated with exact prior knowledge of the perturbation it is being tested against, which the baseline does not have and which no open-world deployment would have. *Why it matters*: this is the single question that decides whether the 34.5 pp gap is a property of the method or of the closed evaluation vocabulary, and it bears directly on the paper's framing around open-vocabulary, unstructured deployment. *Suggestion*: state D's construction procedure explicitly (**required for ICRA acceptance**; one or two sentences). The deciding experiment is a held-out distractor vocabulary — inject categories absent from D and report the same 18-distractor cell (**strengthening**; if the answer to the first request is that D equals the injection list, this experiment becomes required). — **Severity**: Major | **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §III-B Concept-Gated Decomposition — expected a stated derivation or source for the distractor set D; checked §III-B, §III-C, §IV-A, §V` | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: privileged-information leakage in benchmark comparisons; the confound is possible, not established, because the paper is silent
4. **The evaluated pipeline consumes simulator-privileged information, and the ablation does not measure the cost of replacing it**: §III-G uses SimplerEnv's ground-truth robot mask, and §III-G asserts without evidence that "SAM3 can achieve the similar proprioceptive protection." Table II removes robot-mask protection entirely (77.5 → 73.0) rather than substituting the deployable SAM3-derived mask, so it estimates the value of *having* protection, not the cost of *deriving* it without the simulator. Note also that the removal effect itself is imprecise: −4.5 pp carries an interval of [−12.9, +3.9]. ### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Hypotheses
The three questions in §IV are clear, answerable, and well matched to the design: (1) cross-architecture, cross-task clutter robustness; (2) scaling with distractor density; (3) component contributions. Two gaps. First, no hypothesis is stated directionally in advance, and no analysis is designated confirmatory, which leaves the ~62-contrast grid without a pre-specified target (W12). Second, question (3) is posed generally but answered at a single operating point (W5). A useful addition would be an explicit statement, in §IV, of which single contrast the paper stakes its claim on — on my recomputation, the 18-distractor semantic spoon-on-towel contrast is comfortably strong enough to carry that role alone.

#### Research Design
The core design — controlled injection of a manipulated distractor count, crossed with distractor type, task, and architecture, with seed-matched arms — is appropriate and above the norm for this claim class. Internal-versus-external validity is traded sensibly toward internal validity (simulation, controlled injection), and §IV-A defends the choice via SimplerEnv's real-to-sim correlation.

Two threats to internal validity are unaddressed and are, to my reading, the most consequential design findings in this review. Both concern whether the CGVD arm receives information the baseline does not and that no deployment would supply: the underived distractor vocabulary D (W3) and SimplerEnv's ground-truth robot mask (W4). Each has a clean deciding experiment — a held-out distractor vocabulary, and a SAM3-derived robot-mask arm — and each costs roughly 200–400 rollouts at an operating point the authors already run. I want to be precise that neither is established as a confound: the paper is simply silent, and a one-sentence answer may dispose of the first entirely.

A third design question is the comparator set (W11). Comparing against un-augmented pi0 and GR00T establishes that distillation helps; it does not establish that CGVD's pixel-space intervention is preferable to the feature-space and probe-based interventions §I critiques at length.

Finally, a scope condition worth surfacing: the Layer 2 top-component rule presumes a single target instance (W21).

#### Sampling Strategy
The sampling unit is the episode; 200 per cell, structured as 20 episodes × 10 seeds. Two issues.

*Analysis unit.* Episodes are nested within seeds and are described as "independent" (W2). What each seed randomises is never stated (W14), so I cannot tell whether the 10 seeds capture initial-state variation, distractor placement, policy stochasticity, or all three — which determines whether 10 is an adequate number of clusters.

*Precision justification.* No a priori power or precision analysis appears. For the record, n = 200 per arm supports a minimum detectable difference of roughly 14 percentage points at 80% power (two-sided α = .05, normal approximation, p̄ ≈ .5–.6, arms independent); with a seed ICC of 0.05 that rises to roughly 19.5 pp. Table I's grid is therefore powered to detect only its two largest effects, which matches exactly what my interval recomputation found. This is a constructive framing rather than a criticism of the sample size: 200 per cell is a substantial commitment, and the fix is to analyse it in the paired form the design already supports rather than to collect more.

Placement-failure handling at high distractor densities is unstated and could induce a silent selection effect precisely where the headline claim lives (W14).

#### Data Collection
Instrumentation is appropriate: SimplerEnv with a WidowX arm, single fixed third-person camera, matching the Bridge training setup — a sensible choice that avoids an embodiment shift confound. Distractors drawn from RoboCasa [29] and YCB [32] with collision-aware grid placement is a reasonable injection procedure, though the grid resolution and the injection asset list are unreported (W6, W3).

The outcome variable — success — is never operationally defined (W14). SimplerEnv supplies standard predicates, but which predicate, and whether any partial-success or timeout category exists, should be stated. No missing-data or episode-exclusion statement appears anywhere in the manuscript.

Latency measurement (Table III) is reported as three point values with no hardware, no repetition count, and no dispersion (W9).

#### Analysis Methods

*Method selection.* The reported analysis is descriptive: marginal success-rate means per cell. For episode-level Bernoulli outcomes clustered in matched seeds, the appropriate analyses are (in increasing strength) per-seed paired deltas with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test over 10 pairs; Newcombe or cluster-robust intervals on the difference of proportions; or a mixed-effects logistic regression with method as a fixed effect, a random intercept for seed, and distractor count entered as a covariate to test the "gap widens" interaction directly. The third would answer W1, W2, W12, and W15 in one model.

*Assumption checks.* None are reported. The load-bearing assumption is independence, and it is violated by construction (W2).

*Effect sizes and intervals.* Raw percentage-point differences are reported and are a legitimate effect size for proportions; no interval, and no relative measure (risk ratio, odds ratio, Cohen's *h*), accompanies any of them. Per the statistical reporting standards §1.3, this is the central reporting gap.

#### Results Presentation
Tables I–III are clean and readable, and the delta column in Table I is a good choice. Three presentation-level methodological problems.

*Completeness.* The carrot-task regression, a result the paper foregrounds as a trade-off, has no number anywhere (W10). Favourable results get tables; the unfavourable one gets prose. I do not read this as concealment — §IV-B and §V state it plainly, which is to the authors' credit (S4) — but the asymmetry prevents the reader from weighing the trade-off the paper asks them to accept.

*Verifiability.* Fig. 3's distractor settings are unlisted and provably unequal in spacing (W15). I was also unable to reconcile Table II's 43.0 and 77.5 against the corresponding Fig. 3 points, because the figure is available to me only as its caption; if those rows are the same runs, saying so would let readers check the two surfaces against each other for free.

*A free internal reliability check the authors are leaving on the table.* At 0 distractors, the semantic, random, and attribute conditions are physically identical for a given prompt, so the paper contains multiple independent estimates of the same quantity. Reporting their spread would give a direct, assumption-free empirical estimate of run-to-run noise — and would answer most of W1 without any statistical machinery at all. I would prioritise this, alongside the per-seed paired deltas, above everything else in the revision.

#### Reproducibility
This is the weakest dimension. A training-free method is fully specified by its hyperparameters, and almost none are reported (W6): η, r_d, r_s, r_e, the compositing blur σ, SAM3 confidence thresholds and prompt strings, the LaMa checkpoint, the placement-grid resolution, seed values, the SimplerEnv variant, and the pi0/GR00T checkpoints are all absent; only the 0.5 binarisation threshold appears. The instruction parser that produces c_tgt and c_anc — described as "deterministic" and presented as a contribution over LLM-based relevance determination — is never specified algorithmically. The distractor set D is neither listed nor derived (W3). How any of these were selected is unstated (W7). Eq. (3) is undefined for isolated targets (W8), and the robot's role is formally inconsistent across §III-B, Eq. (2), and Eq. (6) (W20). No code, artifact, project page, or hardware statement appears. My honest assessment is that an independent group could not reproduce Table II from §III as written.

The good news is that most of this fits in a few lines of two-column text plus an artifact link, so it need not compete with the results for page budget.

#### Methodological Fallacies Detected
Consulted against the agent definition's checklist and the statistical reporting standards §4:

- **Uncorrected multiple comparisons (§4.5)** — *present*. ~62 baseline-versus-CGVD contrasts, no correction, no declared family (W12).
- **Independence violation / nested data (§1.7, §5.2)** — *present*. Episodes clustered in 10 seeds, described as "independent," analysed as if unclustered (W2).
- **Over-inference beyond data (Step 5)** — *present*, three instances: OOD-target generalisation claimed with no OOD experiment (W17); "maintaining the VLA's native control frequency" contradicted by its own table (W9); "sim-to-real risk is bounded to policy-level transfer" while an unmeasured perception-level channel exists (W4).
- **Post-hoc mechanism for unmeasured or within-noise effects (HARKing-adjacent, §4.2)** — *present*, two instances: the pre-training-distribution explanation for the carrot non-monotonicity (magnitude never reported) and the "non-monotonic variance" interpretation at 1 distractor (both dips within noise) (W13). The adversarial-patch explanation for the mean-color-fill drop is a third, though there the underlying effect is real (W18).
- **Selective emphasis (§4.1)** — *partial*. The largest cells are narrated while the two negative deltas are relabelled as variance, and the abstract omits the reported regression (W22). I do not find selective *reporting*: the adverse results are present in the body.
- **Missing effect sizes and CIs (§4.3)** — *present*. Differences are reported; intervals are entirely absent (W1).
- **No power analysis (§4.4)** — *present*. No a priori precision justification for n = 200 (see Sampling Strategy).
- **Inconsistent table numbers / text–table contradiction (§4.7)** — *present*. §IV-E "negligible… maintaining native control frequency" versus Table III's caption "moderate overhead" and its +32.8% (W9).
- **Unreachable mean, GRIM (§4.7)** — *not established*. All reported rates are reachable at n = 200; Table I's uniform parity is raised as an open question, not a verified inconsistency (W16).
- **Overfitting / test-set adaptation (checklist)** — *possible, unresolved*. The method does not train, but its hyperparameters were selected somehow; no held-out tuning condition is described (W7).
- **Simpson's paradox (checklist)** — *not detected, and partly pre-empted*. The carrot task does reverse the aggregate direction, but the authors report the subgroup rather than pooling it away. Per-seed subgroup checks remain unavailable.
- **Ecological fallacy, survivorship bias, reverse causation, multicollinearity, endogeneity** — *not applicable or not detected*. The analysis unit matches the inference level; the design is experimental with randomised assignment of condition; there is no regression model. Survivorship bias cannot be fully excluded only because the episode-exclusion policy is unstated (W14).

### Questions for Authors

1. **How was the distractor set D constructed?** Specifically: is D a fixed hand-authored list, and does it contain the same object categories that §IV-A injects from RoboCasa and YCB? If the two overlap, CGVD is evaluated with prior knowledge of the perturbation and the baseline is not. If they do not overlap, saying so removes what is otherwise my most serious internal-validity concern. (Bears on W3 and on the generality of 77.5 vs 43.0.)
2. **Can you report the per-seed success rates, or at minimum the per-seed deltas, for Tables I and II?** You already hold matched-seed data. A Wilcoxon signed-rank over the 10 pairs, or a mixed-effects logistic model with a random intercept for seed, would let you keep the claims that survive and would likely rescue some of the six Table I cells that my unpaired recomputation cannot distinguish from zero. What is the observed ICC across seeds? (Bears on W1, W2, W12.)
3. **What are the six distractor-count settings plotted in Fig. 3?** The caption's own arithmetic (19,200 ÷ 200 = 96 points ÷ 16 curves) forces exactly six per curve, and six integer settings cannot be evenly spaced over 0–18. Since the spacing affects how "the gap widens" reads, please list the settings and support the trend claim with a method × distractor-count interaction estimate.
4. **Do Table II's 43.0 and 77.5 rows reuse the corresponding Fig. 3 runs at 18 semantic distractors, or are they independent runs?** If independent, reporting both would give a direct replication estimate of run-to-run variability, which would strengthen the paper considerably at no page cost.
5. **What values were used for η, r_d, r_s, r_e, and the compositing blur σ, and how were they selected?** In particular: were they tuned by observing success rates on the same cells reported in Tables I–II and Fig. 3, or on a separate development condition? (Bears on W6 and W7.)
6. **What is Eq. (3)'s convention when no distractor satisfies IoU(s_i, d_j) > η?** As written the maximum is over an empty set and g is undefined, which propagates into Eq. (4) — and this is the ordinary case for an isolated target, including the entire 0-distractor column of Table I.
7. **How much does replacing SimplerEnv's ground-truth robot mask with a SAM3-derived mask cost?** Table II removes the protection entirely rather than substituting the deployable alternative, so the sim-to-real cost of §III-G's assertion that "SAM3 can achieve the similar proprioceptive protection" is currently unmeasured. Note also that the removal effect itself, −4.5 pp [−12.9, +3.9], is not distinguishable from zero at n = 200.
8. **Why are all 20 of Table I's cells integer percentages when the attainable grid at n = 200 is 0.5 points, and Table II uses half-integer values?** Were Table I's values rounded to whole percent, or is its analytic n different from 200? (I cannot exclude a transcription artifact in the version I reviewed.)
9. **What defines success, how long is an episode, what does each seed randomize, and were any rollouts excluded** — particularly when collision-aware placement fails at 18 distractors?
10. **What hardware produced Table III, over how many repetitions, and with what dispersion?** And how should §IV-E's "maintaining the VLA's native control frequency" be reconciled with 317 → 421 ms, which is a drop from about 3.15 Hz to about 2.38 Hz?

### Minor Issues

Scoped to methodology-section technical items only; I am not compiling a language, grammar, or layout list, which sits with another seat.

- §IV-A declares three distractor types, but the Attribute type is exercised only in Table I (pi0, one task, 0–4 counts) while Fig. 3 covers semantic and random. Stating this coverage asymmetry once in §IV-A would prevent readers from over-generalising the "three distractor types" framing.
- §IV-C's phrase "0–4 random attribute distractors" mixes two of the three defined type names. Suggest "attribute distractors, randomly selected" if that is the intent.
- Table I's caption uses "variance" informally ("non-monotonic variance") while no variance is reported anywhere. Reserve the term for a reported dispersion measure; "non-monotonic differences" would be accurate.
- Table III's caption ("moderate overhead") and §IV-E's body text ("negligible overhead") disagree on the same page about the same measurement.
- §III-B calls the instruction parser "deterministic" but never describes it. Since deterministic parsing without an API is presented as an advantage over [9], one sentence of specification would let readers evaluate the claim.
- §III-C states the vision encoder runs "only once for the initialization frame (t=0), with its masks reused across all frames"; §III-F says the clean scene is "computed once per episode." These are consistent but use different scoping language ("all frames" versus "per episode") — worth unifying so the caching contract is unambiguous.
- §V lists two assumptions ("it relies on two key assumptions") and then presents three paragraphs. The single-target-instance assumption implied by §III-D's top-component rule is a natural third entry.
- Eq. (5) states r_s ≥ r_d but neither value is given, so the size of the protective buffer — arguably the single most safety-critical parameter in the pipeline, since it determines whether the target survives distillation — is unknown to the reader.
