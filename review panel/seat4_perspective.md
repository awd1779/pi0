## Summary Assessment

CGVD is an inference-time perception wrapper that parses an instruction into a protected "safe set" and a distractor vocabulary, segments both with SAM 3, subtracts the safe mask from the distractor mask, and inpaints the remainder with LaMa before handing the result to a frozen VLA. Evaluated in SimplerEnv across pi0 and GR00T over roughly 19,200 episodes with matched seeds, it lifts success from 43.0% to 77.5% under 18 semantic distractors.

Read as a simulation study of attention corruption, this is solid and unusually honest work — the Carrot-on-Plate regression and the Table I anomaly are reported rather than buried, and the cross-validation gating in Eqs. (3)-(4) is a genuine, reusable contribution to a problem practitioners meet daily. Read as the deployment claim the abstract, §I, and §VI actually make, it is not yet supportable. The method's defining operation is deleting physically present objects from what §IV-A confirms is the robot's *only* camera. The manuscript never characterises what a false-positive erasure costs beyond a success-rate delta, never states whether any non-distilled channel exists, never protects any entity outside `{target, anchor, robot}`, and blends every post-`t=0` frame against a background cached before anything could enter the cell. Two inputs the reported gain depends on — the ground-truth robot mask and the distractor vocabulary D — are simulator-privileged or unstated, and the one-sentence real-world substitute for the first directly contradicts the caching argument the latency claim rests on. My recommendation is Major Revision: the empirical core is worth publishing, but the claims must be scoped to what was actually tested, and the two privileged inputs must be either measured or honestly declared.

## Strengths

### S1: Cross-validation gating is a real contribution to a real practitioner problem
Open-vocabulary detectors score each text prompt independently, so a spatula genuinely does return high confidence for "spoon" — this is the single most common source of silent error when SAM/GroundingDINO-class models are put behind a task policy. Eq. (3)'s confidence differential, with negative values deliberately preserved rather than clipped, and Eq. (4)'s composite component score, are a cheap and inspectable remedy that does not require retraining or an external LLM call. The worked spatula/spoon example in §III-D makes the mechanism auditable by a reader. This element is reusable well beyond CGVD and is, in my view, the paper's most defensible original content.
**Evidence Anchor**: `equation: Eq. (3) — the genuineness differential g(s_i) = σ_safe minus max σ_dist, with negatives preserved`

### S2: Negative results are reported rather than suppressed
§IV-B states plainly that CGVD underperforms the baseline on Carrot on Plate and offers a mechanistic reading (contextual clutter as beneficial visual anchoring). Papers proposing a perception filter usually omit the regimes where the filter hurts. This candour materially raises my trust in the rest of the empirical section and gives a practitioner the beginnings of an enable/disable criterion.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-B "it consistently underperforms the baseline in this specific scenario"`

### S3: The ablation isolates the practically decisive lever
Replacing LaMa with a mean-color fill costs 21.0 pp, more than either other ablated component. That single row tells a deployment engineer exactly where the fragility lives: not in the segmentation logic but in the realism of the fill. It is the most operationally useful number in the paper, and it is also the number that should have driven the sim-to-real discussion (see W6).
**Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table II — mean-color fill 56.5% versus full pipeline 77.5%`

### S4: The integration boundary is the right one
Wrapping at the observation interface rather than inside the policy means the module is drop-in against a frozen VLA, removable without retraining, and testable in isolation — all properties that matter enormously when a perception change has to be justified to an integrator. This architectural choice is what makes the safety questions in W1 tractable rather than hopeless: a module that sits on a wire can be given a bypass, a monitor, and a defined degraded mode.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §III "CGVD operates as a perception wrapper around any Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, requiring no fine-tuning or policy modification."`

### S5: Evaluation scale and matched-seed discipline exceed the norm for this class of paper
200 rollouts per data point across 10 seeds, with seeds matched between baseline and treatment, is more evaluation than most inference-time-intervention papers report. It substantially reduces the chance that the headline gap is a seed artifact.
**Evidence Anchor**: `figure: Fig. 3 caption — 200 rollouts per point, 19,200 episodes total, matched seeds between conditions`

## Weaknesses

### W1: Erasing physically present objects from the robot's only camera is a hazard, and the manuscript contains no failure-mode analysis of it

**Problem**: §III-A defines the method's core operation as producing an observation "in which distractors are replaced with background." §IV-A confirms the platform has "a single fixed third-person camera." The manuscript never states whether the distilled observation is the policy's only visual input or whether any non-distilled channel exists downstream; never distinguishes the distilled stream from anything that might inform collision or contact reasoning; and never analyses what a false-positive distractor classification costs. §IV-D confirms the failure is not hypothetical — without the refinement layer, "genuine targets [are] erroneously inpainted out of the scene" — but the consequence is reported solely as a 12.5 pp success-rate delta. Even in the full pipeline, 22.5% of episodes fail at 18 distractors with no failure attribution.

**Why it matters**: A success-rate delta and a hazard are different quantities. If the policy commands motion from an image in which a real object has been replaced by synthesised background, the object is invisible to the policy and the arm may drive through it. On a WidowX in a research lab this is a bent utensil; the same module on a payload-carrying arm, or in a cell that §I explicitly invokes ("unstructured, human-centric environments"), has a different consequence class. The paper currently asks the reader to accept a deployment framing while supplying only task-accuracy evidence.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — add a short paragraph (four to six sentences is proportionate for 6+2 pages) that (a) states explicitly whether the distilled observation is the sole perception channel or whether raw observations remain available to any other consumer; (b) names false-positive erasure of a physically present object as the method's characteristic failure mode, distinct from task failure; (c) scopes the deployment claim in §I/§VI to the tested envelope. **Strengthening / future work** — a bypass or arbitration design in which the distilled stream feeds only the policy's semantic grounding while a raw or geometric channel retains authority over motion limits.

**Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §V Limitations — expected a failure-mode statement covering physically present objects erased from the robot's only camera view; checked §I, §III-A, §III-G, §IV-A, §V, §VI`
**Severity**: Major — this materially weakens the deployment claim and requires substantial rescoping, but the simulation contribution survives intact. It is not Critical alone: it does not invalidate the measured SimplerEnv result, and it reaches rejection-level impact only in combination with W3, W4, and W6.
**Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: hazard analysis of perception stacks on deployed manipulators.

### W2: The static-background cache is treated as a desynchronisation problem when in a shared workspace it is an intrusion problem, and the compositing mask α that governs this behaviour is never defined

**Problem**: §III-F computes the clean scene once and caches it "for all subsequent frames." §III-G then produces every `t > 0` observation by "smoothly blending the live camera frame o_t with the cached clean scene ô_clean using a Gaussian-blurred compositing mask α." α is never defined — not as an equation, not as a procedure, not by stating whether it is fixed at `t=0` or recomputed. This matters because α is the exact operator that decides what happens to anything that was not in the scene at initialisation. §V frames the cache only as accuracy loss ("the cached background will desynchronize"). §III-B's safe set is `{c_tgt, c_anc, robot}`, which contains no human, no co-worker's tool, and no unmodelled obstacle. A related consequence follows from the same gap: the grasped target travels across the image during the task, and unless α excludes the swept region or the robot overwrite covers the held object, the object in flight may be blended toward cached background.

**Why it matters**: If α is spatially fixed from `t=0` (the reading most consistent with the caching argument), then any object entering an α-weighted region afterwards — a hand reaching in, a knocked-over item, a tool set down by a co-worker — is blended toward a background in which it does not exist. That is not degraded accuracy; it is the canonical dangerous failure of a compositing perception stack, and it is invisible to the reported metrics because SimplerEnv episodes contain no intrusions. As written, a reader cannot even determine whether the failure occurs, because the operator is undefined.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — define α explicitly (equation or two-sentence procedure), state whether it is fixed at `t=0`, and add one sentence scoping validated operation to fully static, human-excluded workspaces. **Strengthening / future work** — a novelty/intrusion check that compares the live frame against the cache in α-weighted regions and falls back to the raw observation on disagreement; this is cheap relative to per-frame re-segmentation and directly addresses §V's own "future work will explore real-time mask updating."

**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §III-G "smoothly blending the live camera frame o_t with the cached clean scene ô_clean using a Gaussian-blurred compositing mask α"`
**Severity**: Major — it does not overturn the reported results, which were obtained in a static environment, but it defines the method's true validity envelope and the paper currently states that envelope only partially and only as an accuracy caveat.
**Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: compositing-based perception in shared cells; the α ambiguity is directly readable from the text.

### W3: The real-robot substitute for the ground-truth robot mask is asserted in one sentence, and it contradicts the caching argument that the latency claim depends on

**Problem**: §III-G reads: "In simulation, we use SimplerEnv's ground-truth robot mask boundary that prevents any frame-to-frame compositing jitter. In real-robot deployments SAM3 can achieve the similar proprioceptive protection." That single sentence carries three loads it cannot bear. (a) Table II prices robot-mask protection at 4.5 pp, so the substitute must be *good*, not merely available. (b) The arm moves every frame, so a SAM 3 robot mask must be recomputed every frame — which is precisely the operation §III-C excludes ("the vision encoder is executed only once for the initialization frame (t=0), with its masks reused across all frames") and precisely what §V calls "currently prohibitive for high-frequency, real-time robotic control." (c) The standard real-hardware solution is never mentioned: projecting the robot's silhouette from joint encoders through forward kinematics and a calibrated camera model. That is deterministic, effectively free, jitter-free by construction, and does not consume the camera at all.

**Why it matters**: This is a disciplinary blind spot rather than an error. The manuscript solves "preserve visual proprioception" as a segmentation problem because the pipeline is framed in vision terms — but a robot arm *has* proprioception, in the encoders, and the mask is a rendering problem, not a perception problem. As written, the one component with an explicitly simulator-privileged input has an unmeasured, self-contradicting real-world story, and a reader cannot tell whether real-robot CGVD costs 421 ms per step or 421 ms plus a full SAM 3 pass.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — either measure the per-frame SAM 3 robot-mask cost on stated hardware and report the real-robot step time, or replace the assertion with an honest statement that the per-frame cost is unmeasured. Adding one sentence noting FK-projected masking as the intended deployment path would resolve the contradiction at essentially zero page cost and strengthen the paper. **Strengthening / future work** — a single real-robot episode with an FK-projected robot mask would convert the paper's weakest sentence into a demonstrated capability.

**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §III-G "In real-robot deployments SAM3 can achieve the similar proprioceptive protection."`
**Severity**: Major — it materially weakens the deployability and latency claims and requires new measurement or claim withdrawal, while the simulation results stand unaffected.
**Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: robot masking and hand-eye calibration on deployed arms.

### W4: The provenance of the distractor vocabulary D at deployment time is never stated, and only the safe set is derived from the instruction

**Problem**: §III-B derives `c_tgt` and `c_anc` from the instruction and calls the parsing "deterministic and requires no additional API." But D is defined only as "semantic categories that may appear as clutter (e.g., spatula, fork, knife)" — the instruction contains none of these words, so D cannot come from the parse. §IV-A says distractors are "drawn from RoboCasa [29] and the YCB [32] dataset," which describes scene generation, not the vocabulary supplied to CGVD. Nothing in §III or §IV states whether D is hand-specified per experiment, taken from the simulator's injected-object list, or a fixed closed vocabulary.

**Why it matters**: Each answer tells a different story, and the deployability claim differs sharply between them. If D is the ground-truth list of injected distractor categories, CGVD is given oracle knowledge of exactly what clutter is present — information no deployment provides, and information the baseline does not receive. In a real kitchen or warehouse the clutter vocabulary is open-ended and unknown a priori, and the "no additional API" advantage over BYOVLA [9] partly evaporates if a human or an LLM must author D per scene. This is also the finding whose answer could most change my assessment: an oracle D would move this toward a validity concern about the headline comparison itself.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — one sentence in §III-B or §IV-A stating exactly how D is constructed for the reported experiments and how it would be constructed at deployment. **Strengthening / future work** — an ablation with a fixed, generic tabletop vocabulary (or with D containing categories not actually present, plus categories present but omitted) to show the gain is not contingent on knowing the injected set.

**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §III-B "semantic categories that may appear as clutter (e.g., spatula, fork, knife)"`
**Severity**: Major — the headline 77.5% versus 43.0% comparison's transferability turns on this, and resolving it may require a new experiment; the paper's mechanism and other results survive.
**Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: deploying open-vocabulary prompt sets in the field; I cannot inspect the authors' configuration, so provenance is inferred from prose alone.

### W5: The real-time framing is contradicted by the authors' own latency table, and the measurement hardware is unspecified

**Problem**: §IV-E states CGVD "optimizes for real-time control" and "adds negligible overhead during execution, maintaining the VLA's native control frequency." Table III reports 317 ms for base pi0 and 421 ms for CGVD at `t > 0` — a 32.8% per-step increase, and a drop from about 3.15 Hz to about 2.38 Hz, a 25% loss of control bandwidth. The table's own caption says "moderate overhead," not negligible. Initialisation is 4,914 ms, roughly 11.7 execution steps, which §V calls "negligible compared to the robot's mechanical movement time" — a comparison that is not supported anywhere and is doubtful for tabletop reaches, which typically complete in a small number of seconds. No CPU, GPU, or runtime configuration is stated for any of these numbers.

**Why it matters**: 2.4 Hz closed-loop is below the bandwidth practitioners assume for reactive or contact-rich manipulation and rules out response to dynamic clutter — which is exactly the regime W2 identifies as unhandled. Presenting that as real-time invites a reader to plan an integration the system cannot support. Without hardware, the numbers also cannot be reproduced or scaled to a target platform.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — align §IV-E's wording with Table III (state the 32.8% increase and the resulting rate honestly), replace "real-time" with the measured control frequency, drop or support the "negligible compared to mechanical movement time" comparison, and add the measurement hardware to Table III's caption. This costs almost no space and removes the paper's most easily challenged sentence.

**Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table III — Execution at t > 0 rises from 317 ms to 421 ms (about 2.4 Hz) while §IV-E calls the overhead negligible`
**Severity**: Major — an explicitly claimed contribution property is contradicted by the manuscript's own reported data and requires rewriting plus missing disclosure.
**Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: control-loop budgeting on real manipulators; the arithmetic is directly from Table III.

### W6: The sim-to-real risk is bounded by assertion, and the paper's own ablation predicts the opposite risk ordering

**Problem**: §IV-A argues that because "both core perception components of CGVD SAM3 [13] and LaMa [14] were independently trained and validated on real-world imagery," the "sim-to-real risk is bounded to policy-level transfer, which SimplerEnv [31] explicitly addresses." This reasons from the components' *training provenance* to the *pipeline's* error behaviour on the target domain, which does not follow. Meanwhile Table II shows the VLA is extraordinarily sensitive to fill quality: substituting a mean-color fill costs 21.0 pp, and §IV-D attributes this to "stark, unnatural region boundaries [acting] as adversarial patches to the VLA's ViT backbone."

**Why it matters**: SimplerEnv renders are relatively clean, matte, and geometrically simple. Real cluttered tabletops are textured, non-planar, and specular. LaMa [14] is a 2022 Fourier-convolution model; in my deployment experience its outputs on such scenes exhibit visible seams, texture hallucination, and geometry destruction around non-planar background — the precise defect class Table II shows costs 21 pp. The paper's own strongest ablation therefore predicts that inpainting-quality degradation is the *most likely* real-world failure mode, which is exactly what §IV-A's sentence assumes away. Segmentation behaviour compounds this: prompt sensitivity, category-boundary drift, small-object and specular-surface misses, and temporal instability are all more severe on real imagery than on renders.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — withdraw or substantially soften the sim-to-real bounding claim, and explicitly connect Table II's fill-quality sensitivity to real-image inpainting quality as the leading transfer risk. This turns a weak assertion into a well-evidenced limitation using data the authors already have. **Strengthening / future work** — a small real-image study (real photos, offline, no robot needed) reporting LaMa fill quality and SAM 3 mask stability on cluttered tabletops would substantiate the transfer argument at modest cost; a comparison against a modern diffusion-based inpainter would additionally show whether the 21 pp lever can be improved.

**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §IV-A "ensuring that the sim-to-real risk is bounded to policy-level transfer"`
**Severity**: Major — it removes the paper's principal threat to validity by assertion, and correcting it requires rewriting the transfer argument and possibly new evidence, while the in-simulation claims stand.
**Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: inpainting and open-vocabulary segmentation behaviour on real cluttered imagery; the magnitude of degradation is my professional judgement, not measured here.

### W7: Four operative hyperparameters are named but never valued, including the one that governs how close the pipeline comes to erasing a genuine object

**Problem**: η (the IoU threshold in Eq. (3)), r_d and r_s (the dilation radii in Eq. (5)), and r_e (the robot dilation in Eq. (6)) are all introduced symbolically and never given numeric values. The only numeric parameter disclosed is the 0.5 binarisation threshold. SAM 3 is cited as arXiv 2511.16719, dated roughly four months before this submission, with no checkpoint, version, or availability statement; no LaMa checkpoint is named either.

**Why it matters**: r_s is not an incidental tuning knob — §III-E describes it as the safe-set dilation "that creates a protective buffer," i.e. it is the single parameter that determines the margin between "distractor removed" and "genuine object clipped." Its value is the method's safety margin, and it is unreported and un-ablated. Without η, r_d, r_s, and r_e, the pipeline cannot be reimplemented at the reported operating point, and a practitioner cannot reason about the trade-off between removal completeness and erasure risk. The unpinned SAM 3 version compounds this: a rapidly moving foundation model without a checkpoint makes the result unrepeatable even with the radii.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — report all four values (a single sentence or a small table row), and pin the SAM 3 and LaMa checkpoints/versions. **Strengthening / future work** — a sensitivity sweep over r_s showing where target clipping begins; this is the ablation a deployment engineer would ask for first, and it converts an unstated parameter into a characterised safety margin.

**Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §III-D and §III-E — expected numeric values for η, r_d, r_s, and r_e; checked §III, §IV-A, Tables I to III, §V`
**Severity**: Major — the reported configuration cannot be reproduced, and the parameter governing erasure margin is undisclosed.
**Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: reimplementing segmentation pipelines from published descriptions.

### W8: A segment-then-erase pipeline is evaluated exclusively by downstream task success, with no perception-layer error rate

**Problem**: The manuscript reports success rate throughout and never reports a single perception-level metric: no mask IoU, no safe-set recall, no distractor-removal precision, and in particular no rate at which a genuine target or a non-distractor object was erased. §IV-D establishes that erroneous erasure happens (the refinement ablation exists precisely to prevent it) but quantifies only its end-task consequence. At 18 semantic distractors the full pipeline still fails 22.5% of episodes with no failure attribution between "policy failed" and "perception erased something it should not have."

**Why it matters**: Two things follow. First, the causal attribution of the 34.5 pp gain to *correct* distractor removal is not established — the gain is consistent with correct removal, but also partly with generic visual simplification, and the paper's mechanism claim rests on the former. Second, for anyone deciding whether to run this module, the erasure error rate *is* the number that matters; success rate aggregates it away. In my field, reporting a segmentation-driven intervention without component-level precision/recall would not pass review, and I flag this as a methodological convention worth importing.

**Suggestion**: **Required for ICRA acceptance** — report at minimum one number: the fraction of episodes in the headline 18-distractor condition in which the target or a safe-set object was partially or fully inpainted. The authors' existing runs should permit this from logged masks without new rollouts. **Strengthening / future work** — full mask precision/recall for both channels across distractor counts, and a failure-mode breakdown of the residual 22.5%.

**Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §IV Experiments — expected segmentation precision, recall, or false-erasure rate for the safe-set and distractor masks; checked §IV-A to §IV-E, Tables I to III, Fig. 3 caption`
**Severity**: Major — without it the mechanism claim is supported only by end-task correlation, and supplying it likely requires re-analysis of logged runs.
**Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: segmentation evaluation; I defer to the methodology seat on whether end-task-only reporting is acceptable by this venue's convention.

### W9: There is no fail-safe, abstention path, or deployment-time criterion for when CGVD should be enabled

**Problem**: The pipeline is all-or-nothing: masks are computed, the scene is distilled, and the policy sees the result regardless of how ambiguous the genuineness scores were. There is no confidence gate that passes the raw frame through when Eq. (4)'s top component is only marginally ahead, and no monitor on the composite. The paper simultaneously reports two regimes where CGVD is *worse* than doing nothing — Table I at 1 distractor (78.0 versus 80.0 simple; 69.0 versus 74.0 complex) and the whole Carrot-on-Plate task — with no way for a practitioner to detect at runtime which regime they are in.

**Why it matters**: In safety-case practice the first question about a new module is "what is its defined degraded state?" Here the answer is that there is none: if segmentation is wrong, the policy is simply handed a false scene with full confidence. The reported regressions make this concrete rather than theoretical — the module can actively harm performance and cannot tell you it is doing so.

**Suggestion**: **Strengthening / future work** — add a genuineness-margin threshold below which CGVD passes the raw observation through unmodified, and report the resulting operating curve; even a brief discussion of what a degraded mode would look like would be valuable at ICRA length. A practical enable heuristic derived from the paper's own data (semantic-distractor density high, task not context-dependent) would also help readers.

**Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table I — CGVD below pi0 at 1 distractor on both prompts (78.0 versus 80.0; 69.0 versus 74.0)`
**Severity**: Minor — the absence of a fallback does not invalidate any reported claim; it is a design gap whose remedy improves deployability without changing results.
**Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: degraded-mode design for perception modules.

### W10: It is not stated whether the raw observation is retained, which affects incident reconstruction

## Detailed Comments

### Assumption Audit

- **Explicit assumptions**: §V names two — static background and inpainting fidelity in non-semantic clutter — and both are real and honestly stated. My concern is not that they are absent but that both are framed exclusively in accuracy terms. "The cached background will desynchronize from the physical scene" is true and also incomplete: desynchronisation in a workspace nothing can enter is an accuracy problem; desynchronisation in a workspace something *can* enter is a hazard. A third explicit assumption is stated but not labelled as one: §IV-A's claim that component training on real imagery bounds sim-to-real risk (W6). It functions as a premise, carries substantial weight, and is not listed among the limitations where the reader would look for it.

- **Implicit assumptions**: Four are load-bearing and unstated. (1) *The scene at `t=0` is the scene forever* — everything in §III-F/§III-G depends on it, and it is stronger than the "static background" §V names, because it also excludes novel objects, not just moved ones. (2) *The distractor vocabulary D is knowable in advance* — §III-B's example list arrives from nowhere, and the claim that parsing "is deterministic and requires no additional API" is true of the safe set but silent on D (W4). (3) *Anything not in the safe set is safe to delete* — a set of three entity types is treated as an exhaustive definition of what must remain visible, which excludes humans, unmodelled obstacles, and the workspace's own structure. (4) *Success rate is a sufficient proxy for perception correctness* — this is the assumption behind reporting no component metrics (W8), and it is the one I would most encourage the authors to reconsider, because the paper's own §IV-D discussion shows they already reason about perception errors qualitatively.

- **Paradigmatic assumptions**: The paper reasons entirely within a computer-vision frame: the robot is a source of pixels, proprioception is something to be *preserved in the image*, and the arm's own kinematic state never appears. This is why §III-G reaches for a SAM 3 robot mask rather than a forward-kinematics projection (W3), and it is a clean example of a discipline's default framing selecting a harder solution than the adjacent discipline's default. A second paradigmatic commitment: the paper treats the observation as a *representation to be optimised for a consumer model*, rather than as a *record of physical state*. Both framings are legitimate, but only the first is present, and the second is what generates the hazard, logging (W10), and validity-envelope questions.

### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

- **Parallel research**: The automated-driving safety community has spent a decade on structurally the identical problem — a perception component that is functioning exactly as designed, with no fault, yet produces a hazardous outcome because the situation lies outside its performance envelope. That is the definition of a *functional insufficiency* under ISO 21448 (SOTIF), and it is a far better description of CGVD's failure mode than "desynchronization." The same community's *operational design domain* concept (SAE J3016) gives the authors ready-made vocabulary for the scoping statement W1 and W2 both request: state the ODD (static workspace, no human entry, fixed camera, known clutter vocabulary), and the limitation section becomes a specification rather than a caveat.

- **Borrowing opportunities**: Three concepts would strengthen the paper at low page cost. (1) *Defined degraded state* from functional-safety practice — what the module does when it is unsure, which W9 shows is currently undefined. (2) *Hazard-and-operability prompts* (IEC 61882-style "no / more / less / reverse / other than" applied to the erase operation) — applying just "other than" to "erase distractor" immediately yields "erase non-distractor," which is the finding in W1 and takes one sentence to acknowledge. (3) *Forward-kinematics-based self-masking* from standard manipulation practice, which resolves W3 outright.

- **Methodological borrowing**: From my own field, component-level evaluation of the perception stage (W8) is the single highest-value import — precision/recall on both mask channels, and specifically a false-erasure rate. From safety engineering, a runtime monitor comparing the live frame against the cache within α-weighted regions would give intrusion detection at a fraction of the cost of the per-frame re-segmentation §V correctly rules out. From the uncertainty-quantification literature, distribution-free calibration of the genuineness score would turn Eq. (3)'s raw differential into a thresholdable quantity with a stated error rate — which is exactly what a fallback (W9) needs.

### Practical Impact

- **Real-world application**: If the results hold, the practical offer is attractive: a removable, retraining-free filter that recovers a large fraction of clutter-induced loss for a frozen policy. The integration story (S4) is genuinely good. But a deployment lead reading this paper today cannot answer three questions it must answer before integration: what error rate does the perception stage have, where does the distractor vocabulary come from, and what does the module cost per frame on real hardware without ground-truth masks. Those are W8, W4, and W3/W5 respectively, and all three are answerable within a revision.

- **Implementation feasibility**: Moderate, with two hard blockers and one soft one. Hard: the ground-truth robot mask has no measured substitute (W3), and the reported operating point is not reconstructible from the paper (W7). Soft: 2.4 Hz plus a 4.9 s startup is workable for slow pick-and-place with a static scene but not for reactive or contact-rich work, and the paper should say so rather than describe this as real-time (W5). Unintended consequences a deployment would encounter: false-positive erasure of task-relevant but unlisted objects; blending-out of intruding objects; and, most likely of all, inpainting quality on real textured tabletops falling far short of the render-domain quality that Table II shows carries a 21 pp lever (W6).

- **Stakeholders**: The paper's only considered stakeholder is the VLA policy. Three others have a stake and are absent. *The person in or near the cell*: never protected, never excluded, never mentioned, though §I invokes human-centric environments. *The integrator or safety assessor*: needs the failure-rate and envelope statements the paper does not supply, and would additionally need to know whether the distilled stream is authoritative for motion. *The incident investigator*: needs the raw record (W10). None of these require a full safety case at ICRA length — they require roughly one paragraph in total, which is why I have banded the corresponding findings Major-by-scope and Minor-by-remedy rather than higher.

### Broader Implications

- **Ethical dimensions**: The core ethical question here is not misuse but *epistemic*: this method makes a robot's perceptual record diverge deliberately from physical reality, and the paper presents that divergence purely as an improvement. Any technique of this class should state plainly that it removes information about physically present objects, because downstream consumers — other modules, human operators watching a feed, post-incident analysts — may reasonably assume the image is a faithful observation. I want to be clear that I see no misconduct and no bad intent here; this is a framing gap in an otherwise candid paper, and a sentence fixes it.

- **Social impact**: Modest and mostly indirect at this stage, but worth one line of foresight: inference-time observation editing is likely to spread quickly precisely because it is training-free and drop-in. Establishing the convention *now* that such papers state their operating envelope and their false-erasure behaviour is cheap; retrofitting it after the pattern is widespread is not. This paper is well placed to set that convention, given how honestly it already reports its negative results.

- **Future directions**: The most valuable follow-ups from my angle, in order: (1) intrusion-aware compositing, which converts the static-background limitation from an assumption into a detected condition and is substantially cheaper than the per-frame re-segmentation §V dismisses; (2) FK-based self-masking, which removes the paper's only simulator-privileged component outright; (3) open-vocabulary D construction, which is the difference between a simulator result and a field method; (4) confidence-gated distillation with a calibrated abstention threshold, which gives the module a degraded state.

## Cross-Disciplinary Reading Recommendations

- **ISO 21448 (Safety of the Intended Functionality, "SOTIF")** — the standard covering hazards arising from performance limitations of a correctly functioning system with no fault present. This is the exact conceptual home of CGVD's failure mode, and its "known/unknown, hazardous/non-hazardous scenario" quadrants would give §V a far sharper structure than its current prose. I can attest this standard exists and covers this concept; I have not verified the current edition's clause numbering.
- **ISO 10218-1 / ISO 10218-2 (industrial robot safety) and ISO/TS 15066 (collaborative robot operation)** — relevant only if the authors intend to retain any human-shared framing; otherwise the useful takeaway is the vocabulary for stating an *exclusion* (workspace is not collaborative, human entry is not permitted during operation), which is the cheapest possible resolution to W1 and W2.
- **IEC 61882 (Hazard and Operability Studies, HAZOP)** — the guide-word method. Applying the guide words to the single operation "erase distractor" mechanically generates the failure cases the manuscript is missing. This is a half-day exercise, not a safety case.
- **SAE J3016 (driving automation taxonomy, source of the operational design domain concept)** — cited here purely for the ODD framing device, which is the clearest available template for the scoping statement I am requesting.
- **Hancock, Ren, and Majumdar, "Run-time observation interventions make vision-language-action models more visually robust," ICRA 2025** — already the manuscript's reference [9] (BYOVLA). I raise it not for coverage but because a direct comparison of *failure profiles* rather than success rates would be illuminating: BYOVLA's sensitivity probe is a form of the confidence gating W9 asks for, and the manuscript's own critique ("provides only probabilistic protection") is worth revisiting once CGVD's own false-erasure rate is measured.

## Questions for Authors

1. **Is the distilled observation ô_t the only visual signal reaching any part of the control stack, or does any consumer (collision checking, workspace limits, an operator display, logging) receive the raw o_t?** If ô_t is the sole channel, please say so explicitly and add the corresponding failure-mode statement; if not, please describe the split. This single answer determines whether W1 is a scoping fix or something larger.

2. **How is the distractor set D constructed — hand-authored per experiment, read from the simulator's injected-object list, or a fixed closed vocabulary — and how would it be constructed on a real robot facing unknown clutter?** If D is derived from the ground truth of what was injected, please state this plainly and discuss what the method's performance would be under an open or mis-specified vocabulary.

3. **What is the exact definition of the compositing mask α, and is it fixed from `t=0` or recomputed?** Concretely: if an object enters the workspace at `t = 20` and lands where a distractor was inpainted at `t = 0`, what does the policy see? And when the grasped target travels across a previously inpainted region during the placement motion, is it protected, or is it blended toward the cached background?

4. **What is the measured per-frame cost of the SAM 3 robot mask that §III-G proposes as the real-robot substitute for SimplerEnv's ground-truth mask, on stated hardware?** Given §III-C caches masks after `t=0` and §V calls continuous querying prohibitive, how is per-frame robot masking reconciled with the 421 ms figure — and was forward-kinematics-projected self-masking considered as an alternative that costs nothing per frame?

5. **In the headline 18-semantic-distractor condition, in what fraction of episodes was the target or another safe-set object partially or fully inpainted?** If the mask logs from the reported runs still exist, this number should be recoverable without new rollouts, and it is the number a deployment engineer needs most.

6. **What are the values of η, r_d, r_s, and r_e, and which SAM 3 and LaMa checkpoints were used?** In particular, how was r_s (the protective buffer) chosen, and how sensitive is the false-erasure rate to it?
